# Business & Sustainability Model

How noBed.ai lasts — because a coordination layer only saves lives if it's still running in
five years. This is a **public-health infrastructure** play, not a consumer app. The design
principle: **the public side is always free; the institutions that gain efficiency and the
funders with a mandate to save lives carry the cost.**

---

## Who benefits, who pays

| Beneficiary | Value they get | Do they pay? |
|-------------|----------------|--------------|
| The public / patients | Free bed-finding, web + SMS | **Never.** Free, forever. |
| Hospitals | Decongestion, fewer futile referrals, faster placement | Institutional subscription (tiered by size) |
| National Ambulance Service | Route to real capacity, less fuel & time wasted | Bundled in public-sector agreement |
| GHS / Ministry of Health | System-wide visibility + a planning/analytics tool | Platform + analytics licence |
| Donors / development partners | Measurable reduction in avoidable deaths | Catalytic & pilot grants |

## Revenue streams (in likely order of arrival)

1. **Grants & catalytic funding** *(now → Phase 2).* Global-health and digital-development
   funders (e.g. foundations, bilateral health programmes, innovation challenges). This is
   how a public-good coordination layer gets built and piloted without charging patients.

2. **Institutional SaaS** *(Phase 2+).* Hospitals and hospital groups pay a subscription
   tiered by facility size and type. What they buy: a decongested emergency department,
   fewer wasted referrals, and the daily bed-count work the "Bed Bureau" does by hand, done
   automatically and shared across institutions.

3. **Government / system licence** *(Phase 3+).* GHS/MoH licence the platform nationally
   plus the **resource-allocation analytics view** — the data that reveals where ICU and
   functional capacity is missing. This is a budget line justified by policy value, not a
   feature fee.

4. **SMS at cost** *(ongoing).* SMS is run at or near cost — subsidised where needed — so
   the lowest-connectivity users are never priced out. This is a mission constraint, not a
   revenue line.

## The unit economics thesis (early, honest)

We don't have live cost data yet — this is a prototype. But the structure is favourable:

- **Marginal cost per lookup is tiny.** Serverless compute, a managed database, and SMS at
  aggregator rates. A bed lookup costs fractions of a cedi.
- **The value per avoided event is enormous.** A single avoided futile transfer saves fuel,
  ambulance-hours, ED congestion — and sometimes a life. The ROI case is measured in
  outcomes, not click-through.
- **The wedge is cheap to enter.** We sell *into* the existing, respected Bed Bureau Unit
  role rather than asking hospitals to adopt a brand-new workflow. Lower adoption cost.

## Why this is defensible

- **The network effect is the moat.** noBed.ai is only useful when hospitals are on it — and
  more useful with every hospital added. The first connected region is hard to dislodge.
- **Data trust compounds.** The verification and freshness layer gets more valuable and more
  accurate the longer it runs and the more community reports it absorbs.
- **It rides an existing, trusted role** rather than fighting institutional inertia.

## Impact accounting (the real bottom line)

Financial sustainability is the means; the metric is lives. We track:
- **Time-to-placement** (the north star).
- **Futile referrals avoided** (transfers to genuinely full facilities, prevented).
- **Stale-data rate** (how honest the map is).
- **SMS reach** (users served without a smartphone or data).

A subscription only exists to keep those numbers moving. If the model ever pulls against the
mission, the mission wins — starting with the rule that **the public side is free, always.**
