# Competitive Landscape

Who and what noBed.ai is really up against — and why the gap is defensible.

---

## The honest headline

**The main competitor isn't another app. It's the status quo:** phone calls, WhatsApp
messages, a nurse's memory, and a manual bed count on a clipboard. That's what a dispatcher
uses today when a patient needs a bed. It's slow, unshared, and invisible past one building.

---

## The alternatives, and where they fall short

### 1. Phone calls & WhatsApp (the real default)
- **What it is:** ring around hospitals until one says yes.
- **Where it breaks:** no shared picture, no history, no verification, and "yes" on the phone
  can be "full" on arrival. It doesn't scale past a few known contacts.

### 2. The manual "Bed Bureau Office"
- **What it is:** a hospital unit that counts beds daily to decongest its own ED. The research
  ranked it the **2nd most effective** existing fix.
- **Where it breaks:** it stops at the hospital wall. It has no view of, and no link to, other
  facilities — exactly the inter-institutional gap the study flags.
- **Our stance:** not a competitor — a **customer and a champion.** noBed.ai is the Bed Bureau,
  digitised and connected across institutions.

### 3. Government patient-tracking / records systems
- **What it is:** existing digital records of admissions.
- **Where it breaks:** the research documents them as **inaccurate** — discharged and even
  deceased patients still listed as occupying beds, so the system shows **"full" when beds are
  free.** A record that lies is worse than none in an emergency.
- **Our stance:** we're a real-time **capacity & coordination** layer with a **trust model**,
  not a records system. We can complement them, and our discharge/data-accuracy hooks directly
  attack the "false full" problem.

### 4. Generic map / directory apps
- **What it is:** a list of hospitals with locations and phone numbers.
- **Where it breaks:** static. No live capacity, no functional-capacity logic, no reservation,
  no trust labelling, no SMS. A directory tells you a hospital *exists*, not whether it can
  take your patient *right now*.

### 5. International hospital-capacity platforms
- **What it is:** capacity/command-centre software built for high-resource health systems.
- **Where it breaks:** priced and designed for different infrastructure; typically no
  SMS-first inclusion, no equipment-gated "functional capacity" for low-resource settings, and
  no fit for Ghana's tiered referral reality. Built elsewhere, for elsewhere.

## Why the position is defensible

1. **Network effects.** noBed.ai is only useful when hospitals are on it — and more useful with
   each one added. The first connected region is hard to unseat.
2. **Data-trust compounds.** The verification + freshness + community-report loop gets more
   accurate the longer it runs. That reputation is not quickly copied.
3. **The trusted-role wedge.** Riding the existing Bed Bureau role lowers adoption friction in a
   way a foreign entrant can't easily replicate.
4. **Built for the constraint.** SMS-first, equipment-gated, licence-free maps, serverless
   cost base — engineered for low-resource systems from the ground up, not ported into them.
5. **Honest scope.** We don't claim to fix infrastructure or workforce, so clinicians trust
   what we *do* claim. Trust is the moat that matters most in health.

---

## The risk we respect

The status quo is **free and familiar.** Beating "just call them" means being **faster and
more trustworthy than a phone call** — every single time. That's the bar. The product is built
around clearing it, and the pilot exists to prove we do.
