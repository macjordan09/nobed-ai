### Slide 2 — The moment
2018, Accra. A 70-year-old man is turned away from **seven hospitals** — two private, five
state, including Korle-Bu — and dies. Somewhere in that city, a bed was almost certainly free.
Nobody could see it. *(Citi Newsroom, 11 June 2018.)*

They call it **No Bed Syndrome.** And the worst part: it's often not a bed problem. It's a
**visibility problem.**

---

### Slide 3 — The problem
Three failures, stacked:
1. **No shared, real-time picture** of who has a free bed — between facilities, or even
   between tertiary hospitals.
2. **"Full" is often false** — records list discharged and even deceased patients, so
   hospitals show full when beds are open.
3. **A bed isn't a bed** without the oxygen, ventilator, and staff to run it.

The most-cited *effect*, in the research: **loss of the patient.**

---

### Slide 4 — Why it's real (and big)
- Ghana: **0.9 hospital beds / 1,000** (2021). Africa: **1.3 / 1,000** — lowest of any region.
- **10 of 16 regions had no ICU beds**; **0.5 ICU beds / 100,000**.
- Only **5 teaching hospitals** for 16 regions.

*Sources: WHO GHO 2018; Siaw-Frimpong et al. 2021; Reach Alliance 2024. When beds are this
scarce, coordinating the ones you have isn't a nice-to-have — it's the whole game.*

---

### Slide 5 — The solution
**noBed.ai** — one live, colour-coded, national view of where care is actually available,
on the **web and over SMS**. Hospitals update capacity; the public and ambulances see one
honest map; referrals hold a bed on acceptance.

The coordination and visibility layer on top of the system Ghana already has.

---

### Slide 6 — Why we're different
1. **Never a false green** — every number labelled Verified / Self-reported / **Stale**.
2. **Functional capacity** — equipment-gated beds; the number you can actually act on.
3. **Bed reservation on accept** — a referral that means something.
4. **SMS-first inclusion** — works on a feature phone with no data.

---

### Slide 7 — Independent validation
A 2024 **Reach Alliance** study (University of Toronto's Munk School + Ashesi University)
recommended *"an integrated referral system that makes use of the Bed Bureau Unit's role to
connect the various healthcare system referral levels."*

**That's noBed.ai — named, independently, by academics.** We're not inventing a need; we're
digitising a role hospitals already run by hand (the "Bed Bureau Office" — the 2nd most-cited
effective fix in the study).

### Slide 9 — Business model
- **Public side: free, always.**
- **Institutional SaaS** — hospitals subscribe (tiered by size) for decongestion & automation.
- **Government licence + analytics** — GHS/MoH pay for national visibility and a
  resource-allocation tool.
- **Grants** fund the public-good build and the pilot.
- **SMS at cost** — inclusion is a constraint, not a revenue line.

*(Detail: [Business Model](../executive/BUSINESS-MODEL.md).)*

---

### Slide 10 — Go-to-market
Wedge in through the **existing Bed Bureau role** (low adoption cost), start with **one
region and a hospital cluster + NAS dispatch**, prove time-to-placement, then expand region by
region. Network effects: each hospital added makes the map more valuable.
*(Detail: [Go-To-Market](GO-TO-MARKET.md).)*

---

### Slide 11 — Competition
The real competitor is the **status quo**: phone calls, WhatsApp, and manual counts. Existing
government records are *inaccurate* (the "false full" problem). No connected, real-time,
trust-labelled, SMS-inclusive system exists across institutions.
*(Detail: [Competitive Landscape](COMPETITIVE-LANDSCAPE.md).)*
